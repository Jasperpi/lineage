# Ancestry, ancient-DNA, trait and health-report upload services and small DNA companies (state as of September 2026)

Conventions used below. "Observed 2026-09-25" means the page was fetched directly on that date and the figure was read from it. "(search-result summary)" means the fact came from a search engine's summary of the linked page and the page itself could not be fetched (403, paywall or bot wall). Treat those as lower confidence. "(historical, YYYY)" marks information that predates 2026. Prices are USD unless the seller prices only in EUR. EUR prices are given as listed, not converted. Competitor-authored sources are flagged as such.

## Key Question 1: For each service, what does it claim, which methods and data sources does it state, what does it charge (USD, with the date observed), what does it show visually, and what is its privacy stance?

### Takeaway
The "fun" ancient-ancestry sector (MyTrueAncestry, Illustrative DNA / MyHeritage Ancient Origins, Global25 sellers) sells projections of a consumer SNP-chip file onto a PCA or coordinate space built from published ancient genomes. The results are packaged as tiered, gamified reports with maps, timelines and "closest ancient sample" cards. Few of them publish their sample lists, SNP overlaps or fit statistics. The upload-and-report health and trait sector (Sequencing.com, Genomelink, SelfDecode, Xcode Life, Promethease) sells per-report or subscription access to curated SNP–trait lookups. Prices run from about $5–$99 per app (Sequencing.com, third-party figure) and $25 per report (Promethease) to $319 or more for an upload (SelfDecode). The open and research efforts (openSNP, DNA.Land) are gone and deleted their data. The one exception is a 2017 CC0 openSNP dump that the Internet Archive still hosts. Genes for Good has stopped collecting. Nebula survives only as the "DNA Complete" brand of financially stressed ProPhase Labs.

#### Global25 (G25) / Eurogenes / Vahaduo
- The author describes G25 as a PCA "based on approximately 300,000 SNPs" with 25 principal components. — [Eurogenes blog, 2019](https://eurogenes.blogspot.com/2019/07/getting-most-out-of-global25_12.html)
  - Purpose: "to provide data for mixture modeling… for estimating ancestry proportions, both ancient and modern".
  - Scaled and unscaled coordinate sets exist; "The scaled coordinates produce more stable and realistic results most of the time".
  - Ancient samples are run only if they have ≥15% of the SNPs; below that they get a "low_res" suffix.
  - Modelling is done with the nMonte R script or VahaduoJS.
  - The author's own caveat: "It's difficult to say how accurate these estimates are."
  - The reference datasheets are free on Google Drive and updated "every few weeks". (Historical, 2019.)
- How a personal row is sold now: G25 Requests (g25requests.app), run by "Davidski and teepean47". Observed 2026-09-25. — [G25 Requests](https://g25requests.app/)
  - €15.00 per standard submission; €30–€50 for VCF, BAM, CRAM or FASTQ.
  - "Expect your results by email within seven days"; payment through Stripe.
  - "Your file is deleted after processing. We keep no copy of your genotype data."
  - The page gives no method description and sends users to third-party interpreters (Explore Your DNA, AncestralGenome).
- Historical (2021): the Eurogenes store sold a G25 analysis for $12, free for owners of Global10 coordinates, with VCF/BAM/CRAM/FASTQ conversion at €30–50 extra (search-result summary). — [Eurogenes Genetic Ancestry Project store, Aug 2021](https://bga101.blogspot.com/2021/08/genetic-ancestry-online-store.html?m=1)
- Competitor survey of G25 routes (Ancestrify, updated 2026-09-24). — [Ancestrify: where to buy G25 in 2026](https://www.ancestrify.io/blog/where-to-buy-g25-coordinates)

- Vahaduo is "A collection of tools for admixture and PCA analysis based on multivariate input data". #### openSNP (closed 30 Apr 2025)
- Announced 31 Mar 2025; shut down and all data deleted by 30 Apr 2025. — [Bastian Greshake Tzovaras, "Sunsetting openSNP"](https://tzovar.as/sunsetting-opensnp/)
  - Reason 1: most datasets came from 23andMe users, so after 23andMe's bankruptcy "the potential for adding more data… will be quite diminished".
  - Reason 2: "a rise in far-right and other authoritarian governments".
  - "The risk/benefit calculus of providing free & open access to individual genetic data in 2025 is very different compared to 14 years ago."
  - Deleting the data "feels like it is the most responsible act of stewardship".
  - The post calls police DNA phenotyping a "21st century re-run of long-debunked craniometry".
- openSNP was founded in 2011 by Greshake Tzovaras, Philipp Bayer and Helge Rausch. It had about 13,000 users and about 7,500 genomes at closure, and the data were deleted rather than archived. — [TechCrunch, 1 Apr 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/01/genetic-sharing-site-opensnp-to-shut-down-citing-concerns-of-data-privacy-and-rise-in-authoritarian-governments/)
- A pre-closure copy still exists. The Internet Archive item "OpenSNP Data Dumps" (uploaded 2017-12-08, licence CC0 1.0) holds `opensnp_datadump.2017-12-08.zip` at 21,376,203,875 bytes. The item was still listed and not darkened when its metadata API was checked on 2026-09-25. — [Internet Archive: opensnp_data_dumps](https://archive.org/details/opensnp_data_dumps)

#### DNA "face" products and the forensic pigmentation standard
- **Parabon Snapshot**: claims to predict "genetic ancestry, eye color, hair color, skin color, freckling, and face shape" and to be "used by hundreds of law enforcement agencies". It was developed with DoD funding and is "shown to be extremely accurate". No price or consumer-array support is listed (observed 2026-09-25). — [Parabon Snapshot phenotyping](https://snapshot.parabon-nanolabs.com/phenotyping)
- How Snapshot works and what it costs, per The Intercept (2 Feb 2025). — [The Intercept](https://theintercept.com/2025/02/02/forensic-dna-phenotyping-parabon-nanolabs-police/)
  - Parabon says it uses principal component analysis on data from 1,000+ volunteers (37% self-identified White).
  - A forensic artist finishes each composite, which depicts a "young adult at normal body weight" (BMI 22).
  - It cannot predict hairstyle, tattoos, scarring, dyed hair or weight.
  - Police pay "hundreds per case". About 70 composites are publicly posted.
  - Parabon calls its code "proprietary" and says peer review is unnecessary.
- **Consumer "Face DNA" apps** run in the opposite direction: they guess ethnicity from a photo, not a face from DNA. Examples are "Face DNA: AI Ethnicity Test" on Google Play and "Ancestry & Ethnicity: Face DNA" on the App Store (search-result summary: "AI limited to purely visual analysis"). — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ineptlyawesome.facedna&hl=en_US); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ancestry-ethnicity-face-dna/id6747972810)
- **Research DNA-to-face, Difface** (Chinese Academy of Sciences, Luonan Chen's team; *Advanced Science*, 9 May 2025). — [ZME Science](https://www.zmescience.com/science/news-science/ai-dna-3d-facial-reconstruction/)
  - Trained on 9,674 Han Chinese volunteers with genomes and 3D face scans.
  - Mean error: 3.5 mm from DNA alone; 2.93 mm with age, sex and BMI added.
  - Faces became "more generic" below 70% SNP data. It has not been validated on other populations, and no consumer product was mentioned.
- **Human Longevity face prediction** (historical, 2017): Lippert et al. (PNAS) sequenced about 1,000 people to predict face, voice, height and other traits. Critics are covered under Key Question 3. — [Erlich critique, bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/185330v3); [Lippert et al. response](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/187542v3)
- **HIrisPlex-S webtool (Erasmus MC)** is free. — [HIrisPlex-S webtool](https://hirisplex.erasmusmc.nl/)
  - IrisPlex uses 6 SNPs (eye), HIrisPlex 24 SNPs (eye and hair), and HIrisPlex-S 41 SNPs (eye, hair and skin).
  - Output categories: 3 eye, 4 hair and 5 skin, plus hair shade.
  - Input is 0/1/2/NA allele counts per SNP, or a file.
  - Maintained by Erasmus MC's Department of Genetic Identification with Indiana University's Walsh lab.
  - Warns that "Partial profiles… should be used with caution".
- HIrisPlex-S developmental validation (Chaitanya et al., FSI: Genetics 35:123–135, 2018). — [PubMed 29753263](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29753263/)
  - 41 SNPs in two multiplexes; eye model 6 SNPs, hair model 22 SNPs, skin model 36 SNPs.
  - Complete profiles from as little as 63 pg of DNA.
  - "the first forensically validated tool for skin colour prediction".
- Skin model accuracy (Walsh et al., Hum Genet 2017; 2,025 individuals from 31 populations). — [PubMed 28500464](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28500464/)
  - Cross-validated AUC, 3 categories: Light 0.97, Dark 0.83, Dark-Black 0.96.
  - 5 categories: Very Pale 0.74, Pale 0.72, Intermediate 0.73, Dark 0.87, Dark-Black 0.97.

## Key Question 3: Which claims are scientifically criticised, by whom, with citations?

### Takeaway
Criticism is sharpest for DNA-to-face composites. Named forensic geneticists (Walsh, Shriver), civil-liberties lawyers (EFF, NACDL) and earlier Erlich's 2017 rebuttal of face prediction all say the science does not support individual faces. Ancient-DNA "matches" and coordinate fits are criticised on method: coverage limits for ancient genomes, PCA artefacts, no p-values or model rejection, and the gap between similarity and descent. Some of these caveats are printed by the sellers themselves. Health-upload products are criticised as non-clinical and upsold, and new WGS/embryo entrants such as Nucleus draw strong academic criticism.

### Cited Findings
- **Ancient DNA matching (23andMe/Harvard authors, AJHG, 3 Aug 2023; Harney et al.).** — [Harney et al., AJHG 2023 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10502734/)
  - IBD with ancient genomes can be applied "confidently" only above 1× coverage; results below 1× need caution and nothing below 0.5× was reported.
  - "Approaches that do not account for the unique characteristics of aDNA data have the potential to be highly inaccurate", with both false positives and false negatives.
  - Companies should "not overstate the likelihood… of direct genealogical connections", and "the lack of IBD sharing cannot be interpreted as conclusive evidence that there is no shared genetic relationship".
  - Reports should say "genetic connections are not necessarily more important than other types of connections".
- **Seller-printed caveats.**
  - MyHeritage: results "do not indicate direct descent or family relationships with historical figures". — [MyHeritage blog, May 2026](https://blog.myheritage.com/2026/05/new-in-ancient-origins-discover-your-connection-to-notable-individuals-from-history/)
  - The G25 author: "It's difficult to say how accurate these estimates are", with a warning about overfitting. — [Eurogenes blog](https://eurogenes.blogspot.com/2019/07/getting-most-out-of-global25_12.html)
  - MTA: "does not guarantee the accuracy". — [MTA terms](https://mytrueancestry.com/en/terms)
- **Coordinate and NNLS fits** (competitor critique): "A coordinate fit always returns percentages, has no p-value and cannot reject a model." — [Ancestrify](https://www.ancestrify.io/blog/where-to-buy-g25-coordinates)
- **PCA as a basis for ancestry claims.** Elhaik (Lund University), *Scientific Reports* 2022: PCA outcomes "can be artifacts of the data and can be easily manipulated to generate desired outcomes"; 32,000–216,000 genetic studies may need reevaluation, including ones behind commercial ancestry tests. — [Elhaik 2022, Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-14395-4); [Lund University release](https://www.lunduniversity.lu.se/article/study-reveals-flaws-popular-genetic-method)
- **MTA-specific critique** (Genomelink, a competitor): its ancient samples are not "true reference populations"; some archaeological groups rest on a single individual; there is no disclosed threshold. — [Genomelink blog](https://genomelink.io/blog/mytrueancestry-review)
- **Hype in ancient DNA generally.** Jones & Bösl, "Ancient human DNA: A history of hype (then and now)", *Journal of Social Archaeology* 2021 (title-level citation). — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1469605321990115)
- **Parabon Snapshot face composites** (The Intercept, 2 Feb 2025). — [The Intercept](https://theintercept.com/2025/02/02/forensic-dna-phenotyping-parabon-nanolabs-police/)
  - Susan Walsh (Indiana University Indianapolis, co-developer of HIrisPlex-S): "The science isn't there. We shouldn't be doing it"; "We can't even do a nose right now". She calls face prediction "more science fiction… and less science fact".
  - Mark Shriver (Penn State, former Parabon collaborator): the data set is "far too small to support the kind of individualizing predictions".
  - Jennifer Lynch (EFF): "It's not a real image of a person."
  - Clare Garvie (NACDL) says lack of transparency is the "single biggest issue".
  - Rebecca Wexler (UC Berkeley Law) criticises the use of trade secrets.
  - Kara Moore (Utah) says composites can contaminate witness memory.
  - Edmonton, 2019: a composite of a Black man was withdrawn by police after backlash.
  - Parabon's reply: critics spread "misinformation" and peer review would have delayed the service.
  - Coverage of the same criticism: [Biometric Update, Feb 2025](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202502/use-of-dna-phenotyping-to-solve-crimes-fraught-with-problems-critics-say); [CBC 2018 headline: "Police sketch created with DNA technology is potentially useless or even misleading, says scientist"](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/calgary/police-sketch-dna-1.4547272)
- **Human Longevity face prediction (2017).** Yaniv Erlich's bioRxiv critique (7 Sep 2017) says the method "performs similarly to a simple baseline procedure", uses "technically wrong metrics" and "does not really identify anyone". The authors replied in "No major flaws…". — [Erlich, bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/185330v3); [Lippert et al. reply](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/187542v3); [Nature news headline: "Geneticists pan paper that claims to predict a person's face from their DNA"](https://www.nature.com/news/geneticists-pan-paper-that-claims-to-predict-a-person-s-face-from-their-dna-1.22580)
- **Limits of the validated pigmentation standard.** Walsh et al. 2017 report AUCs of only 0.72–0.74 for the Very Pale, Pale and Intermediate skin categories, against 0.87–0.97 for Dark and Dark-Black. — [PubMed 28500464](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28500464/)

- **Research face models.** Difface was trained only on Han Chinese, is unvalidated elsewhere, and faces turn "more generic" with incomplete SNP data.   - Sequencing.com results "are not clinical", and app quality "varies". — [KnowYourDNA](https://knowyourdna.com/sequencing-review/)

  - SelfDecode says its own upload results are less complete than its kit results (search-result summary), which undercuts its imputation claims for uploads. — [KnowYourDNA SelfDecode review](https://knowyourdna.com/selfdecode-review/)
- **Nucleus Genomics** (new WGS and embryo entrant). — [Wikipedia: Nucleus Genomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleus_Genomics)
  - Arthur Kaplan and James Tabery (*Scientific American*, Dec 2025): "Products do not do what the company claims."
  - Sasha Gusev (Harvard): "not yet clinically actionable".
  - Eric Turkheimer: a "new eugenics compan[y]".
  - Ruxandra Teslo (Cambridge): a "non-rigorous company".

### Inferences
- The best-supported claims are narrow: pigmentation through HIrisPlex-S with published AUCs, and population-level PCA placement. Claims outrun evidence at three points: individual faces, "descent from" named ancient people or civilizations, and single-sample "matches" from low-coverage genomes. That is where a transparent tool can differentiate, by showing coverage, overlap, fit statistics and printed limits.
- Sellers already print caveats, but in fine print. The AJHG authors from 23andMe and Harvard recommend making them prominent and educational. That gives an open tool an evidence-backed standard to follow.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or news critique aimed specifically at MyTrueAncestry or Illustrative DNA was found. Critiques of those two are either generic (PCA, aDNA IBD) or from competitors.
- No published rebuttal of Elhaik 2022 was retrieved, so the debate over his claims is not represented here.
- The primary text of *Scientific American* (Kaplan & Tabery) was not fetched; it is cited through Wikipedia.

## Key Question 4: Which unique datasets do they rely on, and could an open tool use the same public data transparently?

### Takeaway
The ancient-ancestry sellers largely re-package public data. The Allen Ancient DNA Resource (AADR) is CC0. Its v66 release has about 17,600 ancient individuals at up to about 2M SNPs; v66.0 is dated April 2026 per a search summary, and v66.p1 went up on Dataverse on 8 June 2026. The sellers' proprietary layer is curation, labels, the private PCA space (G25) and the interface. An open, in-browser tool can use AADR plus HGDP/1000 Genomes, both fully open, and publish its loadings, sample lists and fit statistics. The one thing it cannot replicate is the private customer-database layer that 23andMe's IBD Historical Matches depend on.

### Cited Findings
- **AADR paper.** Mallick et al., *Scientific Data* 11:182 (2024): a curated compendium of published ancient human DNA at more than a million SNPs, maintained since 2019. It crossed 10,000 individuals at the end of 2022. — [Mallick et al. 2024, Sci Data](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-024-03031-7)
- **AADR on Harvard Dataverse** (queried 2026-09-25). — [AADR on Harvard Dataverse (doi:10.7910/DVN/FFIDCW)](https://dataverse.harvard.edu/dataset.xhtml?persistentId=doi:10.7910/DVN/FFIDCW)
  - Latest dataset version 14.0, released 2026-06-08, containing the v66.p1 files. Licence: CC0 1.0.
  - Individuals are "genotyped at up to an expanded set of 2 million positions"; earlier versions used about 1.23 million.
  - Files: 1240K, 2M, 2M_compatibility, HO and compatibility_HO genotype sets (.geno/.snp/.ind/.anno) and an mtDNA FASTA.
  - Caveat: the AADR genotypes "are not a perfect match to those from the associated published papers". Calls are re-made uniformly by sampling a random read.
- **Own count of the AADR v66.p1 1240K annotation file** (downloaded 2026-09-25), from the same [Dataverse dataset](https://dataverse.harvard.edu/dataset.xhtml?persistentId=doi:10.7910/DVN/FFIDCW).
  - 23,089 rows. 19,118 have a mean date >0 BP (about 17,629 unique individual IDs); 3,970 are present-day.
  - Ancient rows by age: 7,721 under 2k BP; 7,140 at 2–5k BP; 3,923 at 5–10k BP; 334 over 10k BP.
  - By data type: 11,246 1240K-capture (.AG), 6,767 shotgun (.SG), 927 Twist (.TW), 74 diploid (.DG).
  - Top countries: Russia 1,769, Hungary 1,473, UK 1,385, China 957, Austria 878, Italy 780, Germany 770, Spain 693, Denmark 654, France 587.
  - A search-result summary similarly gives "19,119 sequences (17,634 ancient individuals)" for v66.0 of 13 Apr 2026. — [AADR Visualizer information](https://experience.arcgis.com/experience/244dbd2b96604e178d8a313743b86be5/page/Information)
- **Existing AADR map interface.** An ArcGIS "AADR Visualizer" was published in *Bioinformatics Advances* (2025; title-level citation). — [AADR Visualizer paper](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformaticsadvances/article/5/1/vbaf199/8238351)
- **Who uses AADR.**
  - Illustrative DNA's AdmixLab reportedly runs on "AADR v62 and v66 panels" (competitor source). — [Ancestrify comparison](https://www.ancestrify.io/compare/illustrative-dna)
  - HaploMapper claims ">20,000 ancient DNA samples", and DeepAncestry uses 3,578 ancient samples in 585 populations. — [Illustrative DNA](https://illustrativedna.com/); [DeepAncestry](https://illustrativedna.com/deep-ancestry)
- **Modern reference data.** Koenig et al., *Genome Research* 34:796–809 (2024) harmonised 4,094 high-coverage genomes from 80 HGDP and 1000 Genomes populations with gnomAD. It identified over 153M variants, with "unrestricted individual-level public release" and tutorials. — [Koenig et al. 2024 (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.278378.123)
- **G25's reference averages are free, but its projection space is private.**
  - The datasheets are public: "I update the relevant datasheets regularly". — [Eurogenes blog](https://eurogenes.blogspot.com/2019/07/getting-most-out-of-global25_12.html)
  - Only the author can place a new sample: "That space is private to its author… only his G25 Requests portal can place a new sample into it." — [Ancestrify](https://www.ancestrify.io/blog/where-to-buy-g25-coordinates)
- **Private customer databases cannot be reproduced.** 23andMe's IBD work with ancient genomes used a database of "∼12.8 million people", and ">80%" of customers consent to research. The authors themselves describe the reproducibility limits and commit to re-running comparisons on request for up to 7 years. — [Harney et al., AJHG 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10502734/)
- **Open pigmentation models.**
  - HIrisPlex-S's models, SNP lists and free webtool are public. — [HIrisPlex-S webtool](https://hirisplex.erasmusmc.nl/); [Chaitanya et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29753263/)
  - A 2026 *Scientific Reports* paper presents an imputation-based method for eye, hair and skin prediction from low-coverage ancient DNA (title-level citation from search). — [Sci Rep 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-38372-3)
- **Open consumer genotypes.**
  - openSNP's live service and data were deleted in Apr 2025. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/01/genetic-sharing-site-opensnp-to-shut-down-citing-concerns-of-data-privacy-and-rise-in-authoritarian-governments/)
  - The 2017 CC0 dump (about 21.4 GB) remains on the Internet Archive. — [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/opensnp_data_dumps)
  - A curated CC0 set of 2,280 genotypes was built from 3,137 public files. — [bioRxiv 127241](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/127241.full.pdf)
- **SNPedia** content is CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 US, which is non-commercial and share-alike. — [Wikipedia: SNPedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SNPedia)

### Inferences
- An open tool can plausibly match or exceed the "ancient" reference base of MTA and Illustrative DNA using only AADR (CC0) and HGDP+1kGP. Illustrative DNA shows 3,578 curated ancient samples; AADR v66 has about 17,600 ancient individuals.
- An open tool can also publish what these sellers do not:
  - the exact sample IDs and publications behind each label (the AADR .anno has DOIs);
  - per-sample SNP overlap with the user's chip;
  - the PCA loadings, so anyone can reproduce a projection;
  - bootstrap or standard-error bands rather than a single "match".
  
  This is a design inference.
- AADR's 2M/1240K positions only partly overlap consumer arrays of about 600–700k SNPs, and many ancient samples are pseudo-haploid capture data. A transparent tool should show each user how many SNPs actually support each comparison. That is the precise weakness the AJHG coverage thresholds describe.
- Using the Internet Archive openSNP dump is legal under CC0, but it runs against the founders' 2025 stewardship decision to delete. An open project that stresses privacy should weigh that before using it. This is an ethical inference.
- For "faces", the evidence-backed open option is HIrisPlex-S-style pigmentation probabilities with their published AUCs. Face-shape composites lack independent validation, and critics treat them as a harm risk.

### Gaps
- It was not verified how many of HIrisPlex-S's 41 SNPs are present on current 23andMe, AncestryDNA or MyHeritage arrays. That coverage determines whether pigmentation prediction from uploaded files is feasible without imputation.
